# Candidate scoring — Roast Your Fridge

Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, no ties, margin 1–5, both orders.
Brief criteria, in the judge's own order (lexicographic — first differing
criterion decides):
1. Roasts a fridge or a phone in a single line (pass/fail gate — every
   candidate below passes it; all 36 are about a fridge or a phone).
2. Lands a real laugh on a SPECIFIC, recognisable detail of the appliance
   rather than a generic insult.
3. Turns on a twist, a surprise or a double meaning that pays off in the
   last few words.
4. Reads cleanly aloud as one line, with no setup that needs a second
   sentence.
5. Feels fresh rather than a joke everyone who owns a fridge has already
   worn out.

Scoring: manual pairwise in small batches, 1–5 per criterion C2–C5, using
the brief's own lexicographic rule. No live judge calls were available in
this session (no free OpenRouter key with credit) — this is careful manual
pairwise scoring, not a live grade. Lengths verified by script, all ≤200.

Field-avoidance: the brief's own warning plus sibling-comp field titles
(yogurt age, kale, 2am leftovers, door-sensor/ice-maker, "Per my last
notification") and the most worn fridge premises in general (the light,
the hum, expired milk, midnight snacking, door alarm) were treated as
contaminated. Candidates touching those angles were dropped or down-scored
on C5 even when funny.

Winner: #2 — "The butter compartment has held the same butter since 2019.
It has seniority over me now." (89 chars, 89 bytes, 1 line).

Why it wins: the only candidate at 5/5/5/5. The detail is specific and
recognisable (the little covered butter shelf in the fridge door — every
fridge owner has seen it, nobody roasts it); the twist ("It has seniority
over me now") pays off in the last four words, turning a storage fact into
a status joke; it reads cleanly aloud in one breath; and the premise is
fresh — no worn light/hum/leftovers/ice-maker angle, no phone cliché. It
trusts the reader: no explanation of the joke anywhere.
